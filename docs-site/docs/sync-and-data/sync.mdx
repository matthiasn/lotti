# Sync devices

Lotti Sync keeps supported data aligned between your devices. Each device has
its own local database; Sync moves changes through the configured sync account
and catches a device up after it has been offline. Sync does not move the
system of record into a Lotti cloud: the configured Matrix service relays
end-to-end encrypted payloads between your devices while each device retains
its own local copy.

## What syncs, and what stays put

| Syncs between your devices | Stays on the one device |
| --- | --- |
| Journal entries and tasks, and the links between them · categories, labels, habits, dashboards and measurables · AI providers, models and profiles (including deletions) · saved task filters · config flags · light/dark theme choice · device profiles · your Daily OS name · notifications and their read state · task agents and their links · AI usage records (tokens, cost, energy) | Text-to-speech voice and speed · pane widths · AI concurrency limits · agent throttle timers · day-planning exclusions |

Two details worth knowing. Not everything that syncs is journal data: your
device profile and Daily OS name travel as device preferences and are exempt
from gap detection. And derived numbers are never sent — a saved task filter
syncs, but the task counts computed from it are recalculated on each device.

Open **Settings → Sync Settings** to see the setup, health, and recovery
pages. The number beside **Sync Outbox** is work that has not reached the sync
service yet.

## See your devices

**Settings → Sync Settings → Devices** lists every session on your sync
account — not only the unverified ones. The header counts the devices on your
sync server, and **Add device** starts [pairing another one](./add-device.mdx).

<ManualScreenshot
  caseId="sync/status"
  alt="Lotti Devices page with cards for Mission Control Mac and Admiral Pebble's Phone, their verification chips, last-seen dates, and the Stop syncing this device action"
  caption="Each card shows whether the device is verified and when the server last saw it. A device that has not checked in for a long time is marked as probably no longer in use."
/>

Each card carries a **This device** chip for the one you are holding, and
**Verified** or **Unverified** for the rest. An unverified device cannot read
new entries: a banner above the list says how many there are, and each one
offers **Verify**. If a device has not checked in for a while, verifying it
first warns that it may be offline — the emoji check only works while that
device is awake and showing Lotti. Choose **Verify anyway** only when you know
it is.

To retire a device, choose **Remove from sync** on its card. It is signed out
and needs a new pairing code before it can sync again. If the sync server no
longer accepts the password this device has saved, Lotti asks you to confirm
it's you with the current sync account password before it removes anything. A
device that never finished pairing can only be removed. You cannot remove the
device you are on this way, and devices paired the old way — each with its own
account — can be verified but not removed.

When a device you just added finishes verification, a banner says it joined
and offers **Send settings** and **Send message history** right on this page,
so the new device fills up without a detour.

**Stop syncing this device**, below the list, signs this device out of the
sync account. Your entries stay on it, and your other devices are not
affected; syncing again needs a new pairing code. **Technical details** shows
diagnostic information you can copy into a support request.

Before sync is set up, this page shows **Sync your devices** instead, with
**Set up sync** — see [Add a device](./add-device.mdx), or
[Your first device](./first-device.mdx) if no device is connected yet.

## Pair another device

Pairing is its own flow, with a code to compare and two steps that follow
connecting. It has a page of its own: [Add a device](./add-device.mdx).

In short: on a device that is already connected, open **Settings → Sync
Settings → Devices → Add device** and show the code. On the new device, open
the same page, choose **Set up sync** and scan it. Compare the check code on
both screens before connecting — the QR code carries your sync account
credentials, so it goes to your own device and nowhere else.

## Verify the new device

Pairing connects the device; verification confirms that both devices are
talking to each other without an unknown device in the middle.

<ManualScreenshot

  caption="Compare the entire emoji sequence on both devices. Accept only when every image and its order match exactly."

Both screens show seven emoji. Compare them in person or over a trusted call,
and choose **They match** on both devices only when the complete sequence
matches in the same order. If even one emoji differs, choose **They differ —
cancel** and check the account and device names before trying again.

## Name devices and advertise capabilities

**This device** — “Device name and capabilities” — identifies the current
device to the rest of the sync group. Give it a **Device display name** that
distinguishes the physical machine, such as “Mission Control Mac” rather than
“Desktop,” and choose **Save**.

<ManualScreenshot
  caseId="sync/node-profile"
  alt="Lotti This device page for Mission Control Mac with its display name, detected AI capabilities, and known sync devices"
  caption="Clear device names make verification, diagnostics, and device-pinned local inference much easier to audit."

**Detected AI capabilities** lists what this device can provide, such as local
audio transcription or local language-model inference. **Known sync devices**
shows what the other devices most recently published. The listed capabilities
are descriptive; the relevant AI profile must still be configured to use them.

## Understand catch-up and backfill

Normal sync processes changes as they arrive. **Backfill sync** detects gaps in
the sequence received from known devices and requests missing records again.

<ManualScreenshot
  caseId="sync/backfill"
  alt="Lotti Backfill sync page showing inbound queue, missing, and skipped counts, sync statistics, automatic backfill, and advanced recovery"
  caption="Project Waddle has received 2,868 sequence records and currently knows about four missing records across two device IDs."
/>

- **Inbound queue** is work currently waiting to be applied.
- **Missing** is the number of known sequence gaps.
- **Skipped** is work the inbound queue gave up on after it could not be
  processed normally.
- **Automatic backfill** requests missing entries from the last 24 hours and
  is the right default for normal use.
- **Advanced recovery** contains manual actions for an investigation, such as
  **Catch up now** and requesting older missing entries. Use it only after
  ordinary sync and automatic backfill have had time to settle.

A non-zero missing count is not automatically data loss: another device may
still be offline, or the request may already be in flight. Investigate when the
count remains unchanged while all expected devices are online.

## Read Matrix Stats

**Matrix Stats** separates message volume from processing health. It is useful
when data looks stale but the account still reports as connected.

<ManualScreenshot
  caseId="sync/stats"
  alt="Lotti Matrix Stats with sent message counts by event type, Sync Metrics actions, Top KPIs, database apply counts, and signals"
  caption="Sent counts establish whether messages are moving; the database apply counts show what incoming work actually did."
/>

**Sent messages** counts what this device sent, grouped by Matrix event type.
Under **Sync Metrics**, **Top KPIs** shows the rows applied to the database,
the conflicts created, and the inbound events still waiting in the queue. The
**DB Apply** group adds what was ignored as older data, skipped while a base
row was missing, or dropped after retries.

A growing **DB Applied** count with a queue that drains is healthy. If the
queue stays high or drops keep appearing, use **Retry Now** or **Force
Rescan**, then check the outbox and the other device's connectivity. **Copy
Diagnostics** puts the numbers on the clipboard for a support request.

## Inspect the Outbox

The **Sync Outbox** is the local queue of changes waiting to be sent. The
summary at the top says whether everything is synced, how many items are
waiting or sending, and how many couldn't send.

<ManualScreenshot
  caseId="sync/outbox"
  alt="Lotti Sync Outbox filtered to one journal entry that couldn't send, with its attachment details, Retry, Remove, and Retry all"
  caption="An item that couldn't send keeps its payload details, how often it was tried, and its local attachment, so recovery is specific rather than blind."
/>

Filter the list by state:

- **Waiting** shows items waiting their turn and items currently sending.
- **Failed** shows items that couldn't send after their automatic retries.
  They stay saved on this device but are not sent again on their own: once the
  problem is fixed, choose **Retry** or **Retry all**.
- **Sent** shows delivered items, kept for a while for diagnostics.

Use **Retry all** after the underlying connection or account problem is fixed,
or **Retry** on a single item. **Remove** takes an item out of the queue: it
stays on this device but will never reach your other devices, so remove it
only when that is what you want. Removing it is not a substitute for repairing
the corresponding local entry or attachment.

Continue with [Resolve sync conflicts](./conflicts.mdx) when two devices edited
the same entry independently, or [Sync maintenance](./maintenance.mdx) for
database-level recovery tools.
